# COVE · Collaboration brief

COVE is a working computational encoder for representing visual scenes as structured oscillatory patterns. The research objective is to study how visual information can remain interpretable as it moves from images and video into a distributed, time-dependent field.

The encoder combines foveated and peripheral visual features with interacting complex modes across a spatial grid. The browser workbench visualizes the computed state, spectra, coupling, and visual reconstructions. Visitors can inspect individual sites, play and scrub the computed trajectory, and export the numerical output for analysis. An optional participant-specific calibration estimates still-image ERPs.

In a concept-held-out THINGS-EEG2 pilot with two participants, the hybrid ERP model reduced MSE relative to the training-mean predictor by 10.75% and 12.95%. A strong visual-only kernel achieved 10.76% and 13.01%, leaving the incremental contribution of oscillatory features open. The reconstruction benchmark evaluates visual information retained by the code and reports a separate result for refinement with access to the input. The evidence concerns computational encoding and observational prediction.

Potential collaboration areas include independent replication and comparison against stronger visual baselines, collection of larger event-aligned visual EEG datasets, retrospective evaluation of feedback policies, and research interface design for synchronized recording, timing, and field export. Hardware-facing work could start with data interchange and latency characterization in a recording-only or simulated setup. Any interventional proposal would need a separate validation protocol and applicable research review.

A useful first discussion would establish the scientific question, available signals and stimulus metadata, comparison baselines, and a reproducible evaluation endpoint. The workbench and aggregate results provide a concrete starting point.
